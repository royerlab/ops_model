          OPSin
          Optical Pooled Screening
          Interpretability Viewer
          An opsin is the light-sensing protein in your eye's rods and cones — it's what turns light into vision. OPSin does the same for a microscopy screen, turning raw images into a phenotype you can actually see. In a pooled optical CRISPR screen, thousands of perturbations are imaged together in one dish, and each nudges cell morphology so slightly — and so far beneath the noise of everyday cell-to-cell variation — that the effect is nearly invisible to the eye. OPSin distills every cell into a compact fingerprint, finds the ones that carry a real signal, and uses a generative model to re-render a healthy cell and reveal the phenotype each perturbation produces.
          The tabs
          • Top cells — the real cells that show each phenotype most clearly.
          • Traversal — watch a healthy cell morph into the selected phenotype; a slider dials the effect from subtle to exaggerated.
          • Montage — every perturbation's rendered cell on a map, so similar effects sit together.
          • PCs — real cells sorted along the model's main axes of variation.
          • Attention — where the model looked (brighter = more informative).
          • How it works — a visual, step-by-step tour of the method.
